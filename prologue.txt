In the year 2018, Josiah Richards touched down in Japan, his sights set on one man: Tajiri Juko, the elusive Stand user tied to the Camden Church attack back home in England. What started as a lone-wolf hunt for justice twisted into something far grander—a brutal ascent to power in Japan's hidden world of Stands.
Josiah arrived in Morioh under a stormy sky, his Stand Union Crown humming with restrained fury—a shimmering crown of ethereal chains that bound enemies' powers against them. Whispers led him to the outskirts, where he tracked rumors of "The Faceless Opposer," a spectral Stand said to mimic and counter any ability it faced. But Morioh was no quiet town; it pulsed with rival factions vying for control after the chaos of past Stand battles.
Drawn into a back-alley ambush, Josiah faced two cursed spirits loyal to Ryomen Sukuna—Tadai and Kazai, snarling horrors from the Culling Games' fringes. Tadai's explosive cursed projectiles rained down like hellfire, while Kazai's phasing shadows slipped through walls to strike from impossible angles. Lesser men would have fallen. Josiah? He laughed. Union Crown erupted, its ethereal chains latching onto Tadai's blasts mid-air and hurling them back threefold. Kazai's shadows twisted into knots, binding him in his own cursed darkness. In under a minute, both lay exorcised and broken at his feet—vanquished with ruthless ease. Word spread like wildfire: a foreigner had crushed Sukuna's spirits
2021. The coronavirus pandemic struck the world like wildfire, lockdowns chaining billions in isolation as death tolls climbed. In Tokyo's Shinjuku, the King of Curses, Ryomen Sukuna, met his [cataclysmic end](https://jujutsu-kaisen.fandom.com/wiki/Shinjuku_Showdown_Arc)—exorcised in a sorcerers' bloodbath that shook the veil between worlds. Yet echoes lingered.
Three years after Morioh's shadows, Josiah Richards returned to England, his Union Crown tempered by victory. Amid PartyGate's brewing scandal—parties in Downing Street while the nation suffered—he was assigned to the inquiry. A civil servant by day, kingpin by night, Josiah navigated Westminster's lies, his chains ready to bind fresh corruption.
But the investigation twisted him. Unearthing layers of elite hypocrisy—ministers partying while families buried loved ones—ignited a fire in Josiah's soul. The inquiry turned him narcissist, convincing him he alone could shatter the status quo. No longer content with shadows, he envisioned *Union Crown* as the tool to reform Britain itself: binding corrupt leaders, rewriting power's rules. Ambition swelled—Parliament would kneel, or break.
Whispers spread through the highest circles: Queen Elizabeth had secretly been meeting with him, drawn to the outsider's unyielding vision. And everyone loved the Queen—her quiet strength a beacon amid the chaos, approval soaring as she sat alone at Philip's funeral, the nation's perfect symbol of sacrifice. No wonder, widowed and isolated, she sought Josiah to do her bidding: purge the hypocrites she could not touch. In shadowed chambers at Buckingham, she tested his resolve, her regal gaze piercing his bravado. Was he the crown's reckoning... or its undoing?
Boris Johnson, Public Enemy Number One, became Josiah's stepping stone. 62% demanded his head. The nation got it—gift-wrapped by Union Crown.
[Queen Elizabeth passed away at the age of 96 in the 8th of September 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Elizabeth_II), her reign's final chapter closing as the world mourned. Boris's early downfall had been her last quiet victory, but Josiah stood ready in the shadows—his approval at record highs, Union Crown poised for the succession storm. With the Queen gone, who would his chains bind next?
2 years later, fury boiled over into the Riots of '24. Diesel Busters, the firebrand populist of the Cyan Party—Nigel Farage's true successor—swept to power on a tidal wave of disgust. No more Blue Tories, no more Red Labour: the public demanded an alternative. Cyan banners flew over burning precincts as Diesel's rallies turned violent, his chants of "Drain the Elites!" echoing Josiah's old promises. Now the narcissist faced a mirror—would Union Crown bind the new demagogue... or crown him?
Diesel Busters crowned his triumph by appointing Josiah Richards as Cabinet Secretary of the Cabinet Office—the civil service's iron spine, now bent to populist will. But cracks formed fast. Josiah, guardian of impartiality, clashed with Diesel's raw political hammer. "Neutrality serves the nation," Josiah warned, chains itching beneath his suit. "Politics serves the people!" Diesel roared back. Parliament erupted as Diesel branded Josiah "the obstruction"—a relic blocking Cyan revolution. "Snap election!" he thundered, dissolving government to purge the mandarin menace. In desperation, Diesel forged an unholy alliance with Tommy Robinson, the street-level firebrand whose rallies fused with Cyan fury. United, they marched on Westminster, Cyan flags entwined with Union Jacks. Josiah's chains faced their greatest test: bind the alliance... or join it?
But Josiah flipped the board. Siding with Labour's rising star Mariah Banu, he branded Diesel a "traitor committing sedition"—Cyan-Robinson mobs torching the constitution itself. Union Crown worked invisibly: leaked tapes, witness bindings, scandal chains that crushed the alliance at the polls. Mariah swept victory with Josiah's shadow hand guiding every swing seat. Installed as PM, Mariah turned on her maker. Josiah struck first—defeating both her *and* Diesel's remnants in a single night of ethereal chains. Parliament dissolved in chaos, the nation leaderless. From the ashes, Josiah emerged as Interim Leader, Union Crown's golden halo his crown. The narcissist had won: Britain knelt to its kingpin.
